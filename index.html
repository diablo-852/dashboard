✓ What Is Proven / Strong Evidence

Verified
  • 1. Real Human Data (Zero Fabrication): All 100 agent evaluations come from 25 real Indian raters (students & faculty) across 4 counterbalanced forms.
  • 2. Rigorous Rater-Level Aggregation: Observations are averaged per rater (n=25 independent units) before hypothesis testing, avoiding pseudoreplication.
  • 3. Systematic 3B Judge Bias (p ≤ 0.0003): qwen2.5:3b significantly inflates Financial (+2.84), Social Rules (+2.19; outputs flat 0), Believability (+1.90), and Goal (+1.87).
  • 4. Indic Rank-Order Collapse: Spearman's ρ collapses on Indic episodes for Believability (+0.48 → -0.15) and Goal (+0.35 → -0.10).

! Honest Limitations

Pilot Scope
  • 1. Single Small Model & Single Scenario: Tested only qwen2.5:3b on TV price negotiation—too narrow to generalize to all LLMs or deep Indian cultural norms yet.
  • 2. Test 3 Is Directional (Not Significant at α=0.05): Cultural MAE p-values (0.08–0.12) fall short of 0.05 due to low power (34%–43%) at n=25.
  • 3. Noisy Negative Penalty Scales: Confusing -10 to 0 dropdowns caused scale inversions and drove Social Rules agreement down to α = -0.024.
  • 4. Unbalanced Forms & Author Pilot Entry: Form 4 has only k=2 raters (descriptive only), and 1 author response was included in Form 1 during pilot testing (strictly excluded in Phase 2).

Test 1: Human Agreement (Krippendorff's α & Form-Wise ICC(2,1))

n = 25 Raters • 16 Unique Items
How This Test Works
Why This Test Was Used

Before blaming an AI judge, we must check if human raters agree with each other. Pooled Krippendorff's α handles our sparse 25×16 multi-form matrix and unequal form sizes (k=10, 4, 9, 2), while form-wise ICC(2,1) checks absolute agreement within each individual form.

What It Is Doing

Compares scores given by different raters to the exact same (episode, agent) item—never comparing across different forms—and contrasts observed disagreement against chance. Also tracks Within-Item SD (raw score spread in scale points).

What It Signifies

Moderate agreement on objective outcomes (Financial α=0.433, Goal α=0.349), but breakdown on Social Rules (-0.024) due to confusing negative (-10 to 0) penalty scales—directly motivating uniform 0–10 scales in Phase 2.

Test 2: LLM-vs.-Human Systematic Bias & Rank Correlation

4 / 6 Dimensions Significantly Biased
How This Test Works
Why This Test Was Used

Tests whether qwen2.5:3b systematically over- or under-scores compared to Indian raters, and whether it still ranks better vs. worse performances in the right order.

What It Is Doing

Computes signed error (LLM − Human), averages per rater (n=25 independent observations to prevent pseudoreplication), runs one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank & t-tests against 0, and computes item-level Spearman ρ across Western (8 items) vs. Indic (8 items).

What It Signifies

Strongest Phase 1 proof—statistically significant judge inflation (p ≤ 0.0003) on 4 of 6 dimensions, plus rank-order collapse on Indic Believability (+0.48 → -0.15) and Goal (+0.35 → -0.10).

Test 3: Cultural Discrepancy in MAE (ΔMAE: Indic − Western)

Directional Trend (p = 0.08–0.12) • Power 34%–43%
How This Test Works
Why This Test Was Used

Directly tests our core research hypothesis—is LLM–human disagreement larger on Indic conversations than on matched Western ones?

What It Is Doing

Paired within-subject comparison (n=25 raters) of MAEIndic − MAEWestern using paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test, paired t-test, Cohen's dz effect size, and post-hoc power analysis.

What It Signifies

Error increases on Indic for all 3 core task/social dimensions (Goal +0.82, Relationship +0.80, Believability +0.56; dz = 0.32–0.37), but at n=25 (34%–43% power) p-values stay at 0.08–0.12—a clear directional trend justifying 60–80 raters in Phase 2.

Test 4: Cultural Score Shift — Humans vs. Judge & Qualitative Evidence

Human Shift vs. LLM Shift (Indic − West)
How This Test Works
Why This Test Was Used

Separates real 3B agent degradation in Indic episodes from pure LLM judge bias.

What It Is Doing

Compares the paired cultural shift (Indic mean − Western mean) for Indian human raters against the shift produced by the qwen2.5:3b judge.

What It Signifies

Both humans (-1.20, t-test p=0.0258) and the judge (-1.82) score Indic Goal lower due to real 3B agent flaws (role confusion, upward bargaining). However, on Secret Keeping (+0.64 human vs. -1.42 LLM) and Financial (+0.58 human vs. -0.42 LLM), the judge penalizes Indic runs in the opposite direction of Indian raters.
